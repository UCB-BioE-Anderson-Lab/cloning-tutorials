Protocol

  1. Photograph the plates twice — blue-light transillumination and ambient light. Save both; once you have picked, these are the only record.
  2. Tubes or a block. Up to 4 cultures: snap-cap tubes, each labelled with its clone ID. More than that: a 24-well block, labels on the airpore sheet — the wells are identified by position.
  3. Fill each tube or well with 4 mL 2YT + antibiotic — the one on your labsheet. It is not always carb.
  4. Pick 4 colonies from each plate: touch a sterile toothpick to a single colony and drop it in. It stays there.
    • Pick ones that are easy to pick and look healthy — well separated, round and smooth. Big and well isolated is usually best. Contorted or smudgy means water seepage, or two colonies grown into one.
    • Which colonies to choose is set by the experiment — brightest, smallest, largest, red, or deliberately naive. Your labsheet says which.
    • Into a block, fix the order before you start and write it down. Wells go by position, so if your record and the block disagree the results are silently scrambled — nothing errors. Use your labsheet grid if it has one; if not, draw one yourself and keep it with the block. Tubes do not have this problem.
  5. Cover — tubes: caps on loosely so they breathe; block: the airpore sheet — and grow in the shaking incubator, 16–36 h. Fridge by 36 h or they overgrow.
  6. Parafilm the plates, store them upside-down in the fridge — you may need them again.
