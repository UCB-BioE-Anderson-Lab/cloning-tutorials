topic: Bottom-up pathway synthesis
question: |
  What distinguishes the Act Synthesizer-style approach from classical retrobiosynthesis?
choices:
  A: It begins with LCMS peaks and assigns them to unknown molecules.
  B: It simulates transcriptional noise in a genetic toggle switch.
  C: It starts from native metabolites and enumerates reachable products.
  D: It designs primers for every possible CRISPR targeting plasmid.
answer: C
explanation: |
  The synthesis approach in the lecture is bottom-up: start with a source strain's native or available metabolites, then enumerate chemicals that become reachable through known or hypothesized enzymatic reactions. Retrobiosynthesis more typically starts with a target product and works backward.

question: |
  In the synthesis expansion, why are native metabolites assigned to shell 0?
choices:
  A: They are starting chemicals available before adding enzymes.
  B: They are products that require exactly one new enzyme.
  C: They are excluded from the pathway synthesis algorithm.
  D: They are detected only after LCMS peak annotation.

explanation: |

question: |
  A chemical is first reached at shell 6 in a breadth-first synthesis expansion. What is the best interpretation?
choices:
  A: It appears in exactly six different alternative pathways.
  B: It must be produced by six enzymes acting in a single reaction.
  C: It has six LCMS peaks at distinct retention times.
  D: It requires at least six reaction steps from shell-0 metabolites.
answer: D
explanation: |
  In breadth-first expansion, the shell number corresponds to the minimum number of reaction steps needed to reach the chemical from the starting metabolites under the model assumptions. It is not a count of alternate pathways, enzymes in one reaction, or LCMS peaks.

slug: simple_reaction_network_reachability
difficulty: hard
topic: Reasoning through enabled reactions
question: |
  Metabolites A, X, W, Y, and Z are available in shell 0. The reaction corpus contains:

  ```
  1: A + X -> B + Y
  2: A -> F
  3: B -> C
  4: C + W -> D + Z
  ```

  Which reactions must be introduced to enable biosynthesis of D from the available metabolites?
choices:
  A: Reactions 1, 3, and 4.
  B: Reactions 2, 3, and 4.
  C: Reactions 1, 2, and 4.
  D: Reactions 1, 2, 3, and 4.
answer: A
explanation: |
  Reaction 1 converts available A and X into B. Reaction 3 converts B into C. Reaction 4 uses C and available W to make D. Reaction 2 makes F and does not contribute to D in this network, so it is unnecessary for the D pathway.
