slug: retention_time_meaning
difficulty: easy
topic: Retention time in chromatography
question: |
  In LCMS, what does retention time represent?
choices:
  A: The time when a compound exits the chromatography column.
  B: The time required for an ion to fragment during MS2.
  C: The time needed to grow the culture before extraction.
  D: The time when a molecule gains or loses a proton.
answer: A
explanation: |
  Retention time is the time at which a compound elutes from the chromatography column. It reflects interactions between the compound, the mobile phase, and the stationary phase. It is not the culture time, the ionization event, or the fragmentation time.

topic: Ion notation in mass spectrometry
question: |
  In positive-mode mass spectrometry, what does the notation `[M+H]+` mean?
choices:
  A: The molecule has gained one proton and carries positive charge.
  B: The molecule has lost one proton and carries negative charge.
  C: The molecule is neutral but appears at a higher retention time.
  D: The molecule has fragmented into two equal product ions.
answer: A
explanation: |
  `[M+H]+` denotes the molecule M after gaining a proton, producing a positively charged ion. `[M-H]-` would indicate loss of a proton in negative mode. Retention time and fragmentation are different concepts.

slug: ms1_vs_ms2_information
difficulty: medium
topic: MS1 versus MS2 spectra
question: |
  What additional information does MS2 usually provide compared with MS1?
choices:
  A: The culture condition used before extraction.
  B: The thermocycler program used for cloning.
  C: The row and column position of each sample.
  D: The fragmentation pattern of a selected ion.
answer: D
explanation: |
  MS1 measures ions by m/z, while MS2 selects an ion and fragments it to obtain a pattern useful for structural evidence. Culture conditions, thermocycler programs, and sample positions are metadata or build information, not the extra information from MS2.

question: |
  What is the main advantage of higher mass resolution, such as in an Orbitrap measurement?
choices:
  A: It guarantees that every peak has the correct biological annotation.
  B: It distinguishes ions with very similar m/z values more reliably.
  C: It replaces chromatography because retention time becomes useless.
  D: It makes neutral compounds visible without ionization or adducts.
