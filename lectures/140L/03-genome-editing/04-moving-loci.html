Conjugation

For a strain that will not take DNA at all

  • Some organisms cannot be transformed or electroporated, and no amount of technique fixes it
  • Put an oriT on your plasmid — that is the only change to the DNA
  • Transform it into a donor E. coli carrying the RP4 machinery — usually WM3064
  • Mix donor and recipient on a plate, and the plasmid is donated cell to cell
  • Then select for the plasmid and against the donor

Transposons

For putting DNA in the genome when you do not care where

  • Random insertion, and that is the point — one library of strains, each with the payload somewhere different
  • Put terminal repeats on the ends of the DNA you want in
  • Mix with transposase, transform, and it inserts wherever it lands
  • Or encode it on a plasmid and conjugate it in — for a strain that will not transform
  • In human cells the same job is usually viral — lentivirus integrates at random

glgS troubles

Susan did a markerless glgS knockout of MG1655, a second knockout with CmR of the rfaE gene in another MG1655, and then tried to move the rfaE::Cm marker into the glgS knockout strain by P1 transduction. Her products only have rfaE knocked out. What happened?

In PubMed, protein-search each gene, then navigate from the protein to the CDS to the GenBank sequence

Look up where the two genes actually are:

glgS in MG1655   NC_000913  REGION: 3191739..3191939
rfaE in MG1655   NC_000913  REGION: 3195320..3196753

You cannot move one without the other. The fragment that brings rfaE::Cm in brings the donor’s wild-type glgS with it, so selecting chloramphenicol selects for exactly the event that undoes her first knockout.
