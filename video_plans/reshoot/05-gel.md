## Shots

1. **Why we run a gel** (~30 s) — checking that a product formed, that it is ~3583 bp, and
   that there is no nonspecific amplification. Say **analytical, not preparative**: we are
   checking, not purifying.

2. **What a gel is** (~45 s) — hold a pre-made slab. Agarose from seaweed, dissolved in
   1× TAE at 1% w/v, poured with combs, stored in bulk in the fridge. ⚠️ **Show the new
   gel fridge** and name it, since the storage location changed — this is exactly the kind
   of "where is it" detail that only film conveys.

   > **Do not film the agarose being made here.** That procedure is currently documented
   > two different ways and one of them looks wrong — see README issue 2. Gel prep belongs
   > in the Microwave equipment short, once the recipe conflict is resolved.

3. **Run to red** (~30 s) — DNA is negatively charged and runs toward the red positive
   electrode, so load at the black end. Show the rig with the electrodes visible. Smaller
   fragments move faster.

4. **Prepare the samples** (~60 s):
   - 8 µL loading dye (tube labeled `load`) into a fresh PCR tube
   - 3 µL PCR product, mix, quick spin
   - Marker: 8 µL loading dye into a tube of marker (yellow), mix, spin — **one per section**

5. **Set up the rig** (~60 s) — ⚠️ **new rigs; confirm each step still applies:**
   - Cut a slab with enough wells for everyone plus marker plus a few spare
   - Place in the rig, fill with **1× TAE just to cover**
   - Brace against floating if the new rig needs it — the old one used a plastic wedge
   - Write all sample names on a paper strip in loading order and lay tubes out to match

6. **Loading** (~60 s) — the shot that most needs film. P20 set to **9 µL**, not the full
   11. Say why: it avoids drawing an air gap that stops the well from filling. Show a tip
   descending into a well without puncturing it, and then **deliberately puncture one** and
   show sample leaking out the bottom. Say: use another lane or start a new gel.

7. **Run it** (~30 s) — ⚠️ **new power supply.** Lid and leads, **run to red**. Show the
   actual settings on the new supply rather than reciting the old 175 V / ~10 min figure —
   and correct `gel.md` to match whatever it turns out to be. Emphasise that the real stop
   condition is the **blue front 2/3–3/4 down the gel**, not the clock, which stays true
   regardless of the voltage.

8. **Image it** (~45 s) — transfer to the imager, lay the label strip above the gel,
   photograph **through the orange filter with a phone**. Show the filter being held. Name
   the file by date and time (`2022_05_23-10am.png`) and upload to the Drive form.

9. **Reading the result** (~30 s) — one good lane against the marker at ~3583 bp, and, if a
   failed gel is available, one bad lane. What success looks like is worth more than a
   description of it.

## Watch out

- Every hardware detail above needs confirming on the day. Do not narrate a number from
  the old page if the new equipment disagrees with it.
- 9 µL loading from an 11 µL sample is deliberate. If the narration says "load your sample"
  without the number, students will draw the full volume and get air.
- The upload form and filename convention date from 2022 — confirm the link still works
  before pointing students at it on camera.
