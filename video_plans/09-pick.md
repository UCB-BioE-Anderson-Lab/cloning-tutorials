## Why film this

This is a **judgement** video, not a procedure video. The mechanical act is touching a
toothpick to a plate. What students actually need is an eye for which colony to touch, and
that is taught by looking at plates — ideally several, including bad ones.

## Shots

1. **The plate under blue light** (~45 s) — the hero shot. A real plate, blue light,
   range of green intensities. Let it sit on screen. If a second, sparser plate is
   available, show both: the page's own figure contrasts an ideal plate with a minimally
   usable one, and having both on camera calibrates expectations.

2. **What you're looking for** (~60 s) — point at real colonies while saying it:
   - **Bright green** = a strong promoter driving amilGFP
   - **No fluorescence** = probably leftover pJ12 template or an inactive variant
   - The very brightest may look yellowish **even without blue light** — best candidates
   - **Well-isolated and round.** Avoid touching or clustered colonies; avoid elongated or
     smeared ones
   - **Satellite colonies** — point at a real cluster of small white colonies around a green
     one and explain they are untransformed cells growing where the antibiotic ran out.
     Only the green one carries the plasmid. This is the single most confusing thing on a
     first plate and it is almost impossible to describe in words.

3. **The counterintuitive one** (~20 s) — **small, slow-growing colonies may hold the
   strongest promoters.** Point at one. Say: do not skip them. Students systematically
   pick the biggest colony and the page explicitly warns against it.

4. **Marking and labeling** (~30 s) — mark chosen colonies on the plate bottom. Label
   snap-cap tubes for up to 4 clones: `pP6-79A`, `pP6-79B`, and so on.

5. **Picking** (~45 s) — 4 mL of **2YT + Carb** from the fridge into each tube. Touch a
   sterile toothpick to one colony — gently, a light touch, not a gouge — and **drop the
   toothpick straight into the tube and leave it there.** Show that the toothpick stays in.
   That detail surprises people every year.

6. **Grow and store** (~30 s) — cap and into the shaking incubator overnight. Wrap the plate
   with parafilm and put it **upside-down in the fridge** for records.

7. **Document** (~30 s) — photograph the plate under blue light, name it `pP6-79`, upload to
   the Drive folder. Then show the notes table being filled in — *why* each clone was
   chosen ("very green and slow growing"). Say that the reasoning is part of the record.

## Watch out

- **The plate is the whole video.** If the transformation the night before fails, this
  shoot cannot happen. Plate more than one, from more than one transformation.
- Blue light plus an orange filter or shield — check the shot exposes correctly on camera
  before committing to a take. Green-on-blue is easy to blow out.
