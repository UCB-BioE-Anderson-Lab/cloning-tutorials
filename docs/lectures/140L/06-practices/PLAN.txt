WHY IT IS A REBUILD AND NOT A CONVERSION
----------------------------------------
The source deck spent roughly two thirds of its running time on
biosafety, touched intellectual property in one slide, never mentioned
business or economics at all, and closed with thirteen slides of course
admin (all-hands, pP6 closeout, assignments, final project).

The admin slides stay in PowerPoint and are not converted.

Two further problems drove the restructure:

  the biosafety framework underneath the old deck is being replaced.
  The draft NIH Biosafety Policy for Research Involving Biohazards
  retires the NIH Guidelines for Research Involving Recombinant or
  Synthetic Nucleic Acid Molecules.  Teaching the old vocabulary would
  teach a vocabulary on its way out.

  the economics gap is the one JCA named as the real deficiency.  The
  field repeatedly skips elementary business arithmetic — biofuels,
  the 2015 metabolic-engineering wave, and now cell therapy — and a
  practices lecture that omits it is teaching only half the subject.


THE SHAPE
---------
Seven sections.  Every section teaches a METHOD and then hands the room
a hard case to run it on, so the anchor question is the exercise rather
than a conversation starter.  That is why the anchors can be difficult:
the students have just been given the tool.

Discussion slides use the section ground (amber) and the .ask class, so
the deck visibly stops and the room learns within two of them that amber
means nobody is lecturing.  One picture or one scenario, one line of
text, and every angle to push on lives in the note channel, aimed at the
lecturer rather than at the room.

Safety is about a third of the running time rather than two thirds.

THE ANCHOR QUESTIONS
--------------------
01  THE METAGENOMIC CLUSTER
    A soil sample, metagenomic sequencing, no cloning yet, and you want
    to PCR a gene cluster out of it.  What is your biosafety level?
    The naive chain — what organism, what risk group, carry it over —
    is dead on arrival because there is no organism.  What you would
    actually do is BLAST it: even a novel cluster usually has genes
    identifiable as an abstract type, and that type is associated with
    virulent organisms or it is not.  So the grey zone does NOT default
    to RG2.  Part B: now suppose it is already a cloned library in
    E. coli and expressing, so you cannot BLAST your way out.
    The resolution to teach: you are not classifying an agent, you are
    choosing containment for a piece of work.  "Handle at BSL-2 until
    characterised" is not the claim "this is RG2".

02  YOU ARE THE IBC
    Express a beta-lactamase from a clinical isolate in lab E. coli, to
    benchmark a new selection scheme.  Approve, approve with conditions,
    or refuse.  The room will say it makes the E. coli dangerous.  It
    does not — no immune evasion, no iron acquisition, no adhesins.
    The hazard is in the DNA: autoclaving is decontamination, not
    destruction, and plasmid DNA survives it, so what can leave is a
    mobile determinant.  Then the questions that actually decide it:
    novel or characterised sequence, already endemic or not, and what
    the waste route is.  Then the sting — the same cassette is already
    in the freezer from Addgene and nobody filed anything.  Ends on:
    this is IV-3, approvable by one reviewer, are you comfortable?

03  WHAT WOULD THE AI ACTUALLY RUN INTO
    Two slides.  First: an AI and a liquid handler — walk the chain and
    find the chokepoints.  Ordering is screened and refused; piecing it
    from oligos still needs an account, money and an institution; the
    PI reviews designs and runs independent checks; obtaining material
    physically needs a human.  And the gap between a sequence and a
    working organism is enormous — many designs, tissue culture,
    animals, analytical instruments.  The honest finding is that the
    barriers are physical and institutional, not informational.
    Second slide: the humanoid robot at the bench.  Which barriers are
    left?  Ends unresolved on purpose.

04  CLAIM SCOPING — UC v. ELI LILLY
    Regents of the University of California v. Eli Lilly, 119 F.3d 1559
    (Fed. Cir. 1997).  Consider ONLY claim 1 of the '525 patent; keep
    the '740 out or prosecution history buries the lesson.  It is the
    University of California, not Berkeley — inventors at UCSF, patent
    to the Regents.
    Two-step.  Q1: did Lilly infringe?  Element by element, yes, within
    the literal scope — and "having the structure of the reverse
    transcript" is structural, not a method limitation, so chemically
    synthesised DNA does not escape it.  Q2: so does UC win?  No.  The
    claims were invalid for want of written description and the court
    never reached infringement.
    NUANCE TO PRESERVE: generalising past your examples is normal and
    allowed.  The sin was UNSUPPORTED breadth.  One example can support
    a genus when the disclosure reveals a shared structural feature or
    a representative set.  Do not let the room leave with "one example
    never supports a genus".
    Then make it theirs: you characterise Psal-1, induced 50x, and
    claim "a promoter induced by salicylate".  Same shape.

05  THE ARSENIC BIOSENSOR
    A ten-cent bacterial test strip for arsenic in well water; a
    hundred million people are drinking it.  Run the four questions.
    Units enormous; the buyer is a government or donor, not the person
    drinking, so it is procurement with one customer; cost at volume is
    dominated by distribution and training, not fermentation, so the
    biology is not where the money goes; and cost DOES fall with volume,
    which makes it the opposite of CAR-T.
    Then push past the four: Cartagena, because it is a live GMO
    crossing borders — and does it need to be alive at all?  A
    lyophilised cell-free version sidesteps most of the regulation and
    rewrites the science.  Shelf life and cold chain.  Chemical field
    kits already exist at about a dollar.  Who funds five years with no
    equity return, and if it is philanthropy, should it be patented?
    Saved for last: the person has to act on the result, and telling
    someone their only well is poisoned may not be a benefit.

06  SHIGA TOXIN IN THE REGISTRY
    JCA's own, unchanged, now landing after provenance, release and
    access have been covered.

CLOSING BANK, in rough order of bite: the sperm vaccine; fuel versus
food; the Brazilian CDS and who you owe; open-sourcing as a gift to
whoever misuses it; Registry Tuesday, provisional Friday; does fifty
clean years mean safe or lucky.


SECTION CONTENTS
----------------
00  WHAT PRACTICES MEANS
    Rabinow and SynBERC, and the honest admission that the term
    sprawled.  The frame for the day: the five questions anyone outside
    the lab will ask — is it safe, who says so, who owns it, does it
    pay, should we.  Quick opener: which of those can you answer today?

01  RISK, AND WHAT WE KNOW
    The Rumsfeld slide, kept.  Fifty years without an incident, set
    against the fact that an absence of incidents is not a mechanism.
    JCA's own point that we assert a GFP-overexpressing E. coli cannot
    be dangerous without having established the basis for ruling it
    out.  Cambridge 1976 video, as risk argued with no track record.
    Anchor: the metagenomic cluster.

02  IS IT SAFE?  WHO SAYS SO?                               [BUILT]
    Trigger change, drawn.  Who is actually watching (EH&S, CLEB as
    Berkeley's IBC, ACUC, CPHS; NIH above all of it as a condition of
    the money).  How risk is assessed — agent x modification x
    manipulation.  Risk groups as a property of the AGENT; biosafety
    levels as a property of the WORK, which is the distinction that
    dissolves most of the confusion.  Select agents and the toxin
    quantity rule.  Toxins versus virulence factors.  The real Berkeley
    BUA, with the Section III grid being retired.  IV-1 / IV-2 / IV-3.
    Incident handling, including what the individual does.
    Anchor: you are the IBC.

03  SECURITY
    Biosafety versus biosecurity, defined.  Dual use with the Botox
    example.  The policy that is actually in force — the 2026 Stopping
    High-Risk Life Sciences Research policy, DGOF and IROC, and the
    2024 DURC/PEPP policy that was rescinded one day before it would
    have taken effect.  Why screening exists: the smallpox debate and
    the 1918 reconstruction.  How screening works, from JCA's own slide
    — sequence screening against pathogen gene lists, customer screening
    against denial lists, no PO boxes, eight-year record keeping,
    refusal and referral to law enforcement.  The federal framework, and
    the fact that it binds the BUYER rather than the vendor.
    Benchtop synthesizers against the screen-at-the-vendor model.
    Containment by design: crippled strains, auxotrophs, removing
    high-affinity iron transport, kill switches; the Tylenol case;
    gene drives as the extreme.
    NOT A TABLE OF ESCAPE FREQUENCIES.  The point is that containment
    by design is a probability whose failure modes are biological, so
    unlike an engineering control it degrades with population size and
    time: cross-feeding defeats an auxotroph, and a kill switch is
    under continuous selection to break.  The number that carries it is
    arithmetic the room can do — a litre at 10^9 cells/mL is 10^12
    cells, so a loss-of-function event at any ordinary frequency means
    escapees in every flask.  Measured frequencies, where they exist,
    are garnish for the note channel.
    Anchor: what would the AI actually run into (two slides).

04  WHO OWNS IT
    Patentable / patented / free to practise.  Anatomy of a claim —
    preamble, transition (comprising is open), elements, and that
    infringement needs every element.  Species and genus, and the three
    doctrines that get conflated: definiteness, enablement, written
    description.  Then the case, in two steps.  Then Psal-1 as their
    own version of it.  Then what follows in practice: disclosure
    starts the clock and the Registry is a disclosure; trade secret as
    the alternative; BioBrick Public Agreement and open source.

05  DOES IT PAY?                                            [BUILT]
    CAR-T reprised from the Introduction deck.  The money in three rows
    — one dose, the whole category, the delivery chain.  Where the cost
    comes from: the two processes side by side with every step tagged
    per patient or per batch.  Three waves, one mistake.  The back of
    the envelope, four questions, the fourth decides.
    Anchor: the arsenic biosensor.

06  WHO ELSE GETS A SAY
    Perception, and JCA's point about conflicts of interest.  Asilomar
    against Cambridge as engagement done two ways.  Environmental
    release: field trials, Oxitec, drives outside containment.  Nagoya
    and digital sequence information.  Access and equity.  Provenance
    and consent, HeLa.
    Anchor: Shiga toxin.  Spare: the sperm vaccine.


RULES THIS DECK FOLLOWS
-----------------------
PROVENANCE.  Every business, cost and policy figure carries its source
in the note channel of the beat it appears in, inline, so it can be
defended from the podium.  Where a range is contested — CAR-T COGS
estimates scatter from $78k to over $220k depending on what is counted
— the note names both ends rather than picking a flattering midpoint.
Nothing from memory.  Where a claim is chronology rather than a figure,
the note says so and says what still needs checking.

NO BARE PROPER NOUNS.  Any protein, enzyme or committee named on a
slide says what it does in the same breath.  A name is not a mechanism.

ONE SLIDE STAYS ONE SLIDE.  A closing beat that wipes the stage and
replaces it with a panel of text is a second slide wearing the first
one's title.  Sequences accumulate; nothing is taken away.

BUILDS EARN THEIR CLICKS.  A list does not need one click per line.
A build is for when the last line changes what the earlier ones mean.

THE REAL DEFECT: SLIDES WRITTEN TO BE READ
=========================================================
JCA's verdict, 7 Oct: the outline is right, the slides are not.  They
are documents — text to be read — rather than slides to be presented.
More graphics, fewer words.

MEASURED, so the scale of it is not a matter of taste:

    slides WITH a drawn figure (14)      3 to 9 words
    slides WITHOUT one       (40)       85 to 176 words

The deck is bimodal, and that is the useful finding.  Where somebody
drew a figure the slide is already right — "Three waves, one mistake"
is four words.  The 40 text slides are simply the ones nobody got to.
So this is NOT a change of visual language and NOT a rewrite: it is
finishing the language the deck already has, in seq/lib.js.

WORST FIRST, by density and by how visual the argument already is:

    04   144, 96, 109, 151 on the un-drawn ones
    00   the front door, four slides, no figure at all
    03   six slides 95-176 words — LAST, and see the 03 warning above:

argument as a geometry, a comparison, a chain or a quantity?  If yes it
is a figure and the prose is a transcript of the figure.  If it is a
genuine list of unlike things, it stays a list — but then it is a short
one, and the sentences belong in the note channel.

WORKING RULE — COMMIT AND NOTE AS YOU GO
=========================================================
Sessions on this deck get their shell taken away mid-task.  It has
happened more than once, it is not recoverable within that session, and
anything not written down at the moment it was done is lost with it.

So, for every session from here on:

  - COMMIT EACH FINISHED THING ON ITS OWN, the moment it is finished.
    Explicit paths, never `git add -A`.  A session that dies with four
    finished things uncommitted has delivered nothing.
  - WRITE THE OUTCOME INTO THIS FILE as part of that same commit.  The
    plan is the handover; chat is not.
  - DO THE UNCONTROVERSIAL WORK FIRST.  Deck wiring, the index, voice
    and cutting, the economics and ownership sections — all of it can
    be finished without going near the material that trips the filter.
    Spend the session's shell on that, and leave anything touching 03
    for last, or for a session of its own.
  - DO NOT CAT THIS FILE WHOLE.  Reading PLAN.txt end to end pulls the
    containment and screening material into context in bulk and is
    itself one of the things that trips the classifier — it happened at
    the start of the 7 Oct evening session, on the very first command.
    Read the section you need with sed -n, or grep for the heading.

A SECTION FILE
--------------
Copy the head of 02-safety.html.  Page-local <style> is for things only
this section needs, and every rule in it should carry a comment saying
why it exists.

Each slide is:

  <article class="slide">                       white ground
  <article class="slide" data-bg="section">     amber — dividers, .ask
  <article class="slide" data-bg="lecture">     blue  — rare
  <article class="slide" data-seq="name">       driven by a sequence

  <h1 class="small">Title</h1>
  ...content...
  <template class="notes">spoken, verbatim</template>
  <template class="desc">what is on the screen, for accessibility</template>

A sequence slide carries an EMPTY notes template; its per-beat notes and
descs come from the sequence file.

data-build="1" on an element reveals it as its own step.  Builds earn
their clicks: a list does not need one per line.  Use a build when the
last line changes what the earlier ones mean.

Scripts at the bottom, in this order:
  ../../deck.js, lecture.js, seq/lib.js, then each seq file.

Add the new file to lecture.js in the right position.


A SEQUENCE FILE
---------------
  (function(){ "use strict";
  const G = window.PR, C = G.C;
  ... drawing helpers ...
  function paint(v, f){ ...returns one <g>... }
  const FR = []; let acc = {};
  function beat(o){ acc = Object.assign({}, acc, o.s || {});
                    FR.push(Object.assign({}, o, {s:Object.assign({}, acc)})); }
  beat({ on:[], s:{key:1}, cap:"", call:"", note:"...", desc:"..." });
  window.Deck.sequence("name", function(slide){
    const s = G.scene(slide, capY, callY); s.finish();
    return G.run(s, FR, paint); });
  })();

s:{} values are 0..1 and tween between beats.  paint(v) receives the
interpolated values.  dur in ms overrides the 1200 default.

THE ACCUMULATOR IS NOT OPTIONAL.  Every frame must carry every key that
should still be on screen; a key missing from s:{} counts as zero, which
is a silent deletion.  beat() above merges into a running object so a
key can never be lost by omission.  Write beats only through beat().

G.early(v) and G.late(v) split one tweened beat into two halves, for
when A must visibly happen before B inside a single step.

window.PR provides: C, el, text, path, grp, rich, lines, box, arrow,
step, stack, scene, run, early, late.  step() is the process box that
carries a per-patient / per-batch tag as part of the shape.

THE .ask DISCUSSION SLIDE
-------------------------
data-bg="section" plus class="ask".  One picture or one scenario, one
line, and every angle to push on in the note channel, addressed to the
lecturer rather than to the room.  Write the notes as: the answer the
room will give first, why it is wrong or incomplete, the questions that
actually decide it, and one that tests whether they are reasoning or
performing.  See 02-safety.html slide 12 and 05-economics.html slide 7.


THE AUDIT LOOP
--------------
Needs `mkdocs serve` running and Google Chrome installed.  From the repo
root:

  node docs/lectures/tools/check_section.js 06-practices 02-safety.html /tmp/out

It reports steps, median fill, thin steps, clipping, ink in the margin,
channel junk and console errors, and writes a PNG per step.  Then LOOK
at the frames — the auditor cannot see a collision that is merely ugly,
and it cannot see white ink on a white ground:

G.text AND G.lines DO NOT DO RICH TEXT.  Only `cap` and `call` are set
through innerHTML and run through rich(), so only they understand <b>
and <em>.  An SVG <text> drawn by G.text or G.lines gets its content as
textContent, so a <b> in one of those PRINTS ON THE SLIDE, literally,
as "<b>How</b>".  The auditor does not catch it — it is not clipping,
it is not channel junk, and the console stays clean.  LOOK AT THE
FRAME.  To emphasise a word in a drawing, draw it as its own G.text at
its own size and weight; do not reach for a tag.

Unicode character.  The auditor's "channel junk" check catches it.

rich().  Use colour and weight instead.

  THE GENUS SENTENCE — the one the lecture turns on, verbatim:
    "A description of a genus of cDNAs may be achieved by means of a
    recitation of a representative number of cDNAs, defined by
    nucleotide sequence, falling within the scope of the genus or of a
    recitation of structural features common to the members of the
    genus, which features constitute a substantial portion of the
    genus."                                             [approx 1568-69]
    Two sufficient routes, in the disjunctive.  Later canonised en banc
    in Ariad.

  THE PERMISSIVE SIDE, stated by the court itself, which is what keeps
  the room from leaving with "one example never supports a genus":
    "These cases do not compel the conclusion that a description of a
    species always constitutes a description of a genus of which it is a
    part.  These cases only establish that every species in a genus need
    not be described in order that a genus meet the written description
    requirement."                                          [approx 1568]
    And that generic claiming is normal in chemistry:
    "In claims involving chemical materials, generic formulae usually
    indicate with specificity what the generic claims encompass. ...
    Accordingly, such a formula is normally an adequate description of
    the claimed genus."                                    [approx 1568]
    The misreading comes from casebooks quoting only the first half of
    the In re Grimme sentence; the second half is the escape hatch:
    "it may not be necessary to enumerate a plurality of species if a
    genus is sufficiently identified in an application by 'other
    appropriate language.'"                                [approx 1569]

  WHY UC LOST — function, not structure:
    "a generic statement such as "vertebrate insulin cDNA" or "mammalian
    insulin cDNA," without more, is not an adequate written description
    of the genus because it does not distinguish the claimed genus from
    others, except by function. ... A definition by function, as we have
    previously indicated, does not suffice to define the genus because
    it is only an indication of what the gene does, rather than what it
    is."                                                   [approx 1568]

    description of an invention, not an indication of a result that one

WHAT GOES IN A BUILD AGENT'S BRIEF
----------------------------------
Everything.  An agent cannot ask the user a question, so an incomplete
brief produces invented content.  Include:

  1. repo root and deck directory
  2. "Read docs/lectures/AUTHORING.txt and this file, HOW TO BUILD A
     SECTION, before writing anything."
  3. "Read 02-safety.html and seq/02-ibc.js as the pattern."
  4. the section number, title, slide list and time budget, copied from
     SECTION CONTENTS above
  5. the anchor question in full, with the angles for the note channel
  6. which source pptx slides to draw on, from the slide map
  7. the facts it may use, pasted from FACTS ALREADY SOURCED, plus:
     "Anything not in that list must be marked VERIFY in the note
     channel.  Do not cite from memory.  Do not invent a figure."
  8. "Run the auditor and LOOK at the rendered frames before reporting.
     Report: step count, median fill, anything you could not source,
     and anything you decided that the plan did not specify."
  9. "Do not touch lecture.js."


WHAT THE OVERSEER CHECKS ON REVIEW
----------------------------------
Mechanical, from the auditor:
  no clipping, no channel junk, console clean, step count inside the
  section's time budget at roughly 45-60 s per step.

By eye, from the frames — the auditor cannot see any of this:
  collisions that are merely ugly; white ink on white; a figure that
  rendered at its intrinsic size; a sequence beat that reads as a
  deletion because a key was dropped; a discussion slide that has
  become a reading.

By reading the note channels:
  every number has a source, inline;
  contested ranges give both ends;
  nothing is a bare proper noun;
  the anchor's notes contain the wrong first answer, the counter, and
  at least one question that tests whether the room is reasoning.

Against the plan:
  did it build the section that was asked for, at the length asked for,
  or did it quietly grow?  Growth is the common failure — sections come
  back 50% over budget because every fact seems worth a slide.
