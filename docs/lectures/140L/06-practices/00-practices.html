Practices

BioE 140L · UC Berkeley

Everything about this work that is not the work: safety, oversight, security,
ownership, money, and the people who never set foot in the lab.

Where the word comes from

In the early years of SynBERC — the Synthetic Biology Engineering Research Center, which carried human practices as a thrust alongside its engineering ones — Paul Rabinow, a Berkeley professor, put that phrase into the vocabulary of genetic engineering.

It has been shortened to practices and stretched ever since. Practitioners now use it for biosafety and biosecurity, intellectual property, government regulation, public perception, and education.

Notice what is not on that list: whether any of it can be paid for. That omission is a whole section of this lecture.

Five questions anyone outside the lab will ask

  • is it safe what we actually know about risk, and how work is contained — including whether someone could misuse it
  • who says so the committees, and the federal policy that is replacing the NIH Guidelines this month
  • who owns it patents, and what a claim actually covers
  • does it pay the arithmetic the field keeps declining to do
  • should we everyone who is not in this room — and the only one of the five that no amount of evidence settles
