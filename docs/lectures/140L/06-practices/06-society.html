Who else gets a say

Everything so far has been a decision
made by people inside the building.

Who is best placed to judge

The people best equipped to assess the risk of this work are the people whose careers depend on it continuing. Including me. Including, shortly, you.

  • This is not an accusation. It is a structural fact about expertise, and there is no version of the field in which it is not true
  • It is also the entire reason outside review exists — not because scientists lie, but because nobody is a disinterested judge of their own work
  • Set it beside where we started: we assert that this work is safe from habit rather than from an established basis — and we are the ones doing the asserting

Two ways of being engaged with

Asilomarthe field convened itself

  • Scientists called a meeting of scientists, paused some of their own work, and wrote the containment practices they would then be held to
  • The question on the table was how do we do this safely
  • The field kept the terms. It also kept the credit for having asked

Cambridgea city convened the field

  • An elected city government held hearings on whether this work could be done inside the city limits, and did not ask the universities’ permission to hold them
  • The question on the table was should this be done here
  • The room was residents and councillors. The scientists were witnesses, not conveners

Same technology, same decade, same arguments — and the two produced different kinds of rule. Who is in the room decides what the question is allowed to be.

Release takes the frame away

  • Every control in this lecture — containment levels, waste routes, crippled hosts, kill switches — assumed you could draw a boundary around the work
  • A field trial deletes the boundary on purpose. The organism is not escaping; the organism is the product, and dispersal is the mechanism
  • The case worth holding in your head: engineered mosquitoes released to suppress a wild population — where the release is not a risk to be minimised but the thing you are paying for

The review question stops being can it get out and becomes who agreed to live with it. Containment had a perimeter you could inspect. Consent has a map.

A drive is designed to spread

  • In the security section a gene drive was the far end of a scale: containment failing by design. From this side it is not a failure at all — spreading is the specification
  • An ordinary released organism has to earn its spread by being fitter. A drive is built so that it does not have to, which is what makes it an instrument rather than an experiment
  • So there is no boundary to argue about, and no second decision later. The people who get a say have to get it before the release, because afterwards there is no one to appeal to

Whose material is it

The most widely used human cell line in biology — HeLa — was taken from a patient during treatment, without her consent. Her family found out what had happened decades later.

  • Every hand-off after the first one was completely ordinary: a culture shared, a stock deposited, a vial shipped, a line used. Nobody along that chain did anything unusual, and nobody asked again
  • “It was in the repository” answers where you got it, not how it got there. Provenance is a property of the material. It does not reset when the material changes hands

The sample came from somewhere

Do you see any problems with that?

A student on an iGEM team made a basic part out of Shiga toxin from enterohaemorrhagic E. coli, as a component of a cancer-killing device, and sent it into the Registry. iGEM is the international undergraduate synthetic biology competition; the Registry of Standard Biological Parts is the open collection of parts that any team, anywhere, can order from.

Nobody in this story did anything they thought was wrong.

What we are not going to settle

Every question left has a defensible answer on both sides. That is not a failure of the lecture. It is what makes these practices questions rather than technical ones — and it is why somebody other than you ends up in the room.
