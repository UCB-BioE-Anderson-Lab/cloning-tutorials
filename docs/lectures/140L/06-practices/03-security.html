Same bench, same organism, different adversary

Biosafetythe adversary is error

  • Protects people and the environment from the work
  • A slipped pipette, a tube nobody labelled, an aerosol nobody saw
  • Controls are engineering and habit: containment, technique, training

Biosecuritythe adversary is intent

  • Protects the materials, and the knowledge, from people
  • Theft, diversion, misuse — by an outsider or by the person who already has the key
  • Controls are access, accountability and screening

A biosafety cabinet protects a careless worker from an aerosol. It does not slow down a careful one who wants the aerosol.

Dual use

  • Botulinum neurotoxin is a protease made by Clostridium botulinum. It cuts the proteins a nerve ending uses to release its signal onto a muscle, so the muscle stops being told to contract
  • In a wound or in food that is paralysis, and it kills by stopping the diaphragm. In minute doses in a clinic it is a treatment for spasm, and a cosmetic
  • It is the same protein, the same gene, and the same production problem. The knowledge that makes one makes the other

Dual use is not a feature of a technique that a better technique could remove. It is a property of understanding how something works.

Why anyone screens an order at all

  • Smallpox. Eradicated in the wild. The world then argued for decades about whether to destroy the last stocks of the virus — an argument about freezers, and about who holds a key
  • The 1918 influenza virus. Sequenced out of preserved tissue, and then rebuilt. A virus that no longer existed was made again from its sequence

While the argument about the freezers was going on, the sequence was published. A freezer can be emptied. A publication cannot be unpublished — and at that moment the control problem moves from the vial to the order form.

The ground moves

What a synthesis provider actually does

  • Sequence screening. The complete sequence of every order of 200 bases or more, against a restricted pathogen database and against the public sequence databases — translated in all six reading frames and screened as protein, so that a recoded gene does not walk past a nucleotide match
  • Automation is a filter, not a decision. Every potential hit goes to a human expert
  • Customer screening. Shipping address, institution, country, phone, email, checked against federal denied-party lists — sanctions, export debarment. No residential addresses. No PO boxes. A regulated sequence goes only to a real institution, with a written statement of intended use that is verified independently
  • Records kept at least eight years — a number chosen to line up with the statute of limitations for use of a weapon of mass destruction
  • Refusal, and referral. A member may refuse any order, tell the other members, and call law enforcement — with whom they keep standing relationships

International Gene Synthesis Consortium, Harmonized Screening Protocol v3.0, 3 September 2024, sections 4 and 7.

Who the rule actually binds

  • The federal framework for screening nucleic acid synthesis orders imposes no obligation on the companies that synthesise DNA
  • It runs through the purchaser. Federal funders require that synthetic nucleic acids and benchtop synthesis equipment bought with federal life-sciences money be bought from providers who adhere to it — a condition written into the terms of the award, in force since April 2025
  • Providers join by publicly attesting that they comply. It is an opt-in

“At the time of this publication, no country in the world requires Providers or Manufacturers to screen the sequences they are asked to synthesize.” The International Gene Synthesis Consortium, about itself and its own industry. Harmonized Screening Protocol v3.0, section 4.

The box on the bench

  • A benchtop synthesiser makes the DNA in your own lab. There is no order, so there is nothing for the order screen to look at
  • The federal answer is not to re-screen orders. It moves the chokepoint into the device: the manufacturer screens every customer for legitimacy rather than screening orders, and controls proprietary reagents that only its own machine can use

That is a different trust model, not the same one further down the chain. It rests on device integrity, on firmware, and on a reagent you cannot buy anywhere else.

Today — 13 October 2026 — three things change: the screening window drops from 200 bases to 50; a sequence of concern no longer has to come from a regulated agent, only to contribute to pathogenicity or toxicity; and benchtop machines should screen inside the instrument.
