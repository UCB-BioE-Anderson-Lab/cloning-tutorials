Five places a protein can be

In a nutshell: how hard is each one?

DestinationHow you get there
Cytoplasm Happens by default — translation is already there

Sec pathway secretion

The signal (pre) sequence

also “signal sequence”, also “leader sequence”

  • 18–30 amino acids in length
  • One or more basic residues near the N-terminus
  • A central hydrophobic core of 7 residues or more

Signal peptide prediction

Periplasmic expression

Leader peptidases

  • LspA processes prolipoproteins — major outer membrane components
  • LepB, “signal peptidase” or SPaseI, cleaves most other proteins

Prepro sequences

  • Use parts from well-expressed periplasmic proteins — pelB, ompA, ompT are the common ones
  • Some proteins are toxic in the cytoplasm and fine in the periplasm
  • Some need the oxidative environment to fold — antibodies, for instance
  • And it is a precursor to outer membrane localization or secretion

Outer membrane localization

Beta barrel-type proteins

  • OmpA, OmpT, OmpG, LamB
  • Start with sec secretion to the periplasm
  • Then spontaneously insert into the outer membrane

Autotransporters / two-partner secretion

  • Ag43 is the well-studied example
  • Often a cleaved “passenger” — type V secretion
  • A dedicated secretion system for a single protein
  • Also begins with sec secretion

Extracellular secretion

In gram positives, like B. subtilis

  • No second membrane to get past
  • So prepro targeting results in extracellular secretion directly

Type I, II and III secretion

  • One set of genes assembles the pore apparatus
  • That apparatus transports a set of substrates through the channel
Periplasmic expression

I want to express alkaline phosphatase from Thermus thermophilus (CAC83793.1) in the periplasm of E. coli. Do I need to alter the CDS to get proper localization?

No. It already carries a signal peptide — it is periplasmic in its own organism too, and the sec pathway is conserved enough to read it.
