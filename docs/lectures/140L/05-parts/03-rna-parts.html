RNA-Based Parts

Parts that do their work as RNA — and the reason parts are hard

Same RBS, new gene, no protein — why?

What a ribosome binding site actually is

→A stretch of the messenger that base-pairs with the 3′ end of the 16S ribosomal RNA.

mRNA   5′-AAAGAGGAGGAAAACATAUG ...  3′
            ||||||
16S    3′-AUUCCUCCACUAG             5′

So the site is a competition: pair with the ribosome, or pair with itself. That is why context dependence was never avoidable.

How bad is it?

So stop treating them as two parts

→If the site’s behaviour depends on the gene behind it, then the site and the gene are one object.

Define the transcript as the part: the site and the coding sequence, characterised together, in one tube.

And two things follow: a bacterial gene arrives with a 5′ region evolution already selected, and a gene now costs two parts instead of three. Both of those are §5.

Terminators

→A hairpin in the transcript that makes the polymerase let go. The DNA is where you put it; the RNA is what does it.

  • Intrinsic — a GC-rich stem followed by a run of U’s. Nothing else needed.
  • Rho-dependent — a protein catches up with the polymerase and pulls it off.
  • Efficiency varies — a terminator is not a full stop, it is a probability. Read-through past a weak one is the commonest reason a downstream gene is on when you did not ask for it.

Which is the same problem: it works by folding, so its efficiency is context dependent too. Every part on this level has this.

RNAs that are not messengers

→An RNA can sense, bind, regulate and catalyse. Everything here is a job you would expect a protein to do.

  • Riboswitches — a stretch of messenger folds around a metabolite and changes its own transcription or translation. A sensor with no protein in it — the metabolite-to-RNA arrow from §1.
  • Aptamers — the same trick, selected in vitro for a ligand of your choosing rather than found in a genome.
  • Small regulatory RNAs — ryhB in E. coli pairs with target messengers and marks them for degradation. Iron-responsive, under Fur. Trans-encoded, and it needs Hfq — not simple antisense.
  • Suppressor tRNAs — recode a stop codon as an amino acid, so UAG stops meaning stop in that cell.

A suppressor tRNA is the odd one: it changes the meaning of a codon for the whole cell, not for your construct. Every UAG in every gene.
