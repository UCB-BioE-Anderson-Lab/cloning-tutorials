CONVENTIONS CARRIED OVER FROM THE WHOLE-CELL DECK
-------------------------------------------------
Read that deck's PLAN.txt before touching this one; it was settled the
hard way and the same rules apply here.
  - A NOTE IS WHAT HE SAYS.  First person, students addressed as "you",
    no stage directions.  Source notes are verbatim; a note may be
    SPLIT across clicks but never reworded, and the split is proved by
    concatenating it back.
  - SECTION DIVIDERS ARE A TITLE ON THE AMBER GROUND, with no picture.
    A source image on a coloured ground is a white box and reads as a
    mistake.
  - FIGURES MUST PICTURE REAL THINGS.  No shape standing for an idea.
  - MEASURE PAINTED WIDTH, not the <img> element: nearly every figure
    is object-fit:contain in a flex box, so the element is as wide as
    its column while the picture is letterboxed to the box's height.
  - Readings invented for an illustration are labelled illustrative.

TWO SETS OF SLIDES ARE DRAWN, NOT CROPPED
-----------------------------------------
SOURCE 5-9, because they are motion paths.
The one place the literal pass could not be literal.  Source 7, 8 and 9
are MOTION PATH animations: the cell's contents travel out to an Extract
zone or down to a Debris Pellet, and WHICH contents go WHERE is the whole
argument of each slide.  LibreOffice renders a motion-path shape where it
STARTS, so a render of those slides is every object sitting in its home
position with the "Extract" label lying across the cell.  There is no
still to crop, and variants.py cannot help: it deletes shapes that arrive
later, which captures entrances and nothing else.

So the bacterium is drawn once, in seq/cell.js, and its parts move.  One
drawing serves five slides (cell5 .. cell9).  The contents, the labels,
the destinations and the order are the source's; the notes are verbatim.

SOURCE 43, because its shapes share names.  It builds the ELISA sandwich
in six clicks, a layer at a time — but several of its shapes carry the
same cNvPr name, so deleting "the shapes that arrive later" deletes the
wrong ones: three of the seven states render empty and one jumps two
layers.  There is no intermediate state to crop.  seq/elisa.js redraws
it, layer for layer, and the six labels are HTML beside it rather than
type baked into a bitmap.

SOURCE 35-38, because of the colour.  These are JCA's own drawings and
they convert cleanly in SHAPE — but they were drawn for the grey master,
in a pale blue (#8AB0DD) and a pale yellow that read on dark grey and
nearly vanish on the white ground this deck uses.  Cropping carries that
colour over.  They also build across ten states between them, including
an EXIT on 38 (the unreacted hot tyrosine washing out of the paper),
which no render can give you.  seq/aminoacyl.js redraws them in the house
palette, shape for shape: the synthetase is still a pac-man, the tRNAs
are still cloverleaves, the hot tyrosine is still one dot, the TCA paper
is still a tan disc.

A slide that also carries a list arriving on its last click (7, 9) or a
closing line (8) keeps that in HTML beside the stage: cell.js writes
data-step on the slide and the section's CSS reads it, because a slide
with data-seq has its data-build attributes ignored by deck.js.

TWO DELIBERATE DEPARTURES FROM VERBATIM, both proved by concat.py:
  source 4  the image credit ("Image from handling-solutions.eppendorf
            .com/...") came out of the spoken note and onto the slide as
            a .src line, which is the deck convention for credits.
  source 9  one period moved across a click boundary.  The source reads
            "...and precipitates <click>."; a spoken note that opens with
            a bare period is nonsense, so the click falls after it.  The
            concatenated text is character-identical.

CHECKING THE NOTES
------------------
  python3 ../../tools/verify_notes.py <unpacked-pptx> *.html
  python3 ../../tools/verify_notes.py <unpacked-pptx> --seq seq/cell.js \
          S6=6 S7=7 S8=8 S9=9
  python3 ../../tools/verify_notes.py <unpacked-pptx> --seq seq/aminoacyl.js \
          S35=35 S36=36 S37=37 S38=38
It concatenates each slide's note pieces and diffs them against the pptx.
Everything passes; the one standing difference is source 9's period,
above.  Slides driven by a sequence keep their notes in the sequence
file, so the HTML pass names them and the --seq pass checks them.

from the first: the source shows it from the start too, and variants.py
could not prove that because two shapes on that slide share a name.
