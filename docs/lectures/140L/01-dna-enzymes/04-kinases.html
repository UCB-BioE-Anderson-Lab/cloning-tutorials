T4 Polynucleotide Kinase (PNK)

  • Transfers ATP’s terminal γ phosphate onto a free 5’ hydroxyl of DNA or RNA
  • A synthetic oligo ends in a bare 5’-OH; a restriction cut already leaves a 5’ phosphate — so oligos need PNK and digests do not
  • That phosphate is the licence to be ligated: no 5’ phosphate, no bond
  • It is also the licence to be degraded — λ exonuclease will not start on a 5’-OH end
  • Fastest on single-stranded 5’ ends; blunt and recessed duplex ends need more enzyme and longer
  • With [γ-32P] ATP the same reaction end-labels an oligo
T4 PNK phosphorylates a free 5’-OH. Where on this molecule could it act?
→ Nowhere — every 5’ oxygen here is already bonded on into the chain. PNK needs an end.
