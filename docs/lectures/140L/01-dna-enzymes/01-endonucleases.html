Endonucleases

Endo = within  ·  Nuclease = enzyme that cleaves DNA

  • Restriction endonucleases
  • DNAses
  • Homing endonucleases
  • Cas9

The first two are this section. Homing endonucleases and Cas9 are endonucleases too, and they come later in the course.

Restriction Endonucleases

  • Type I
    • RMS systems (3 subunit proteins)
    • Typically named HsdR, HsdM, and HsdS
    • Generally not sequence specific
    • Not used in vitro; usually knocked out
  • Type II
    • Cleave at specific DNA sequences
    • We’ll go through these in detail
  • Type III
    • Sequence-specific, but not in common use
  • Type IV
    • Cleave modified (methylated, etc.) DNA
    • McrA, McrBC and Mrr, usually knocked out

Type II Restriction Endonucleases

  • Sequence-specific, and the shape of that sequence is not an accident

Type II Restriction Endonucleases

  • 5’ 4bp overhang
    • BamHI G/GATCC
    • XbaI T/CTAGA
    • EcoRI G/AATTC
  • 3’ 4bp overhang
    • PstI CTGCA/G
  • 5’ 2bp overhang
    • NdeI CA/TATG
  • 3’ 2bp overhang
    • PacI TTAAT/TAA
  • Blunt cutters
    • EcoRV GAT/ATC
    • PvuII CAG/CTG
  • Others
    • 5bp, 3bp, and 1bp overhangs
    • SfiI GGCCNNNN/NGGCC
  • Degenerate cutters
    • XcmI CCANNNNN/NNNNTGG
    • AlwNI CAGNNN/CTG
    • FalI (8/13) AAGNNNNNCTT (13/8)

Type IIS Restriction Endonucleases

  • Recognise one sequence, cut a fixed distance away from it: BsaI, BsmBI

Type II General Notes

  • Every one comes from a restriction/modification system, so every one has a partner methyltransferase
  • So the strain you grew the plasmid in can silently block a digest: dam and dcm methylation, tabulated by NEB (more in the methyltransferase section)
  • Star activity: specificity degrades with too much enzyme, glycerol above ~5%, the wrong (low-salt) buffer, or an overnight digest
  • Different enzymes prefer different buffers, many work in PCR buffer
  • All generate clean 3’OH and 5’-phosphate ends

DNAse I

  • Non-specific endonuclease, a very potent enzyme
  • Products have 5’ phosphates and 3’ hydroxyls, so they are competent for ligation on both ends

Uses:

  • Removing DNA from an RNA sample
  • Chopping large DNAs into oligonucleotide-length fragments, such as for DNA shuffling
  • Footprinting, finding where a protein sits on a DNA

Naming restriction enzymes
