The Kinases / Phosphatases

One phosphate, on or off a 5’ end

  • A 5’ phosphate is a licence to be ligated
  • A restriction cut leaves one. Oligo synthesis does not.
  • Kinases put it on; phosphatases take it off

Alkaline Phosphatases

  • Catalyze the hydrolysis of phosphate esters non-specifically
  • Any exposed phosphate: 5’ ends of RNA or DNA, dNTPs, NTPs
  • They cannot tell your vector from your insert — which matters later

Why you phosphatase a vector

Three phosphatases, one difference that matters

  • Phosphatase that survives into your ligation strips the phosphates you just paid for
  • CIP — Calf Intestine Alkaline Phosphatase
    • Oldest, cheapest, most robust; the one every protocol names
    • Notoriously not killed by heat — you must column-purify or extract it away
  • rSAP and Antarctic Phosphatase
    • A short heat step inactivates them cleanly, so you can ligate in the same tube
    • Also the basis of exo-SAP: SAP destroys leftover dNTPs, Exonuclease I the leftover primers

T4 Polynucleotide Kinase (PNK)

  • Transfers a single phosphate onto the 5’ hydroxyl of an RNA or DNA
  • The source is the gamma phosphate of ATP — the terminal one
  • Used on synthetic oligonucleotides so that they can be substrates for ligase
  • Fastest on single-stranded 5’ ends; blunt and recessed duplex ends need more enzyme and more time
  • Also puts a defined or radioactive 5’ end on an oligo, using [gamma-32P] ATP
