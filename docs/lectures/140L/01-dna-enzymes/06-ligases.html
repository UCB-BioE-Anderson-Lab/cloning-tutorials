Ligases

  • Join a 5’ phosphate to a 3’ hydroxyl
  • Requires energy — the reaction is not favorable on its own
  • Not glue. It is a list of requirements, and every one of them is a way to fail

T4 DNA Ligase

  • No homolog in E. coli — the phage brought its own
  • Requires a 5’ phosphate and a 3’ hydroxyl, directly abutting
  • Requires ATP — one per bond. Tired buffer is a leading cause of dead ligations
  • No gap and no mismatch at the junction
  • Any matching ends will ligate — any overhang length, blunt included. (Efficiency falls off as the overhang shortens, and a 1 nt overhang is the worst case of all, worse even than blunt.)
  • The workhorse for joining ends made by restriction enzymes

A nick, not a gap. And held in register.

The other two ligases

  • E. coli DNA Ligase — NAD+, not ATP
    • The housekeeping ligase of repair and replication, present in every cell
    • Seals nicks and cohesive ends — it was the original cloning ligase
    • But it will not ligate blunt ends, which is why T4 displaced it
  • Taq DNA Ligase — the same enzyme, thermostable
    • From Thermus aquaticus; also NAD+-dependent
    • Hot enough to refuse a mismatched junction, most sharply when the mismatch sits at the 3’-OH — the base whose hydroxyl has to do the attacking. That is the basis of the Ligase Chain Reaction and of ligation-based SNP detection
    • It is the ligase in Gibson assembly, alongside the exonuclease and the polymerase that fills the gaps
