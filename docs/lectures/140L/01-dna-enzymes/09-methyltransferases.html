Methyltransferases

Enzymes that move one methyl group onto a base

The sequence does not change. Who is allowed to act on it does.

Restriction–modification: self versus non-self

You are buying half of an immune system

  • The writer’s chemistry is not fixed: m6A, m5C, or m4C. The BamHI methyltransferase uses N4 of cytosine
  • So genomic DNA from an organism running its own R–M system can arrive already protected at the site you wanted, and it will not cut

What the writer actually writes

  • One carbon, carried in on S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)

Your plasmid arrives already annotated

  • E. coli K-12 lab strains run two methyltransferases of their own: Dam, the A in GATC, and Dcm, the inner C in CCWGG
  • Neither is about restriction. Dam is the strand-discrimination signal for mismatch repair and it times replication initiation
  • So every miniprep from DH5α, TOP10 or XL1-Blue is methylated at all of those sites, and nothing in your plasmid map shows it

XbaI, when TC follows the site

          m
5’-gcTCTAGATCat-3’
3’-cgAGATCTAGta-5’
           m

TCTAGA+TC spells a GATC, so Dam has already been there and XbaI is blocked. BclI is worse: TGATCA contains GATC outright, so it never cuts a dam+ prep at all.

Two ways out, and only one of them is cheap

  • Design around it first. NEB tabulates Dam/Dcm/CpG sensitivity for every enzyme — move the two flanking bases so the overlap never forms, or pick an isoschizomer that is not blocked
  • Otherwise change host. Prep from dam− dcm− JM110 or SCS110 — but without Dam, mismatch repair cannot tell the new strand from the old, so these are mutator strains. Grow only what the digest needs, then put the plasmid back into a normal strain
