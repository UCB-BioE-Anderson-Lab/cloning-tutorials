Phosphatases

Water attacks the phosphate. The 5’ phosphate comes off — and a licence is revoked.

Alkaline Phosphatases

  • Hydrolyse phosphate esters — non-specifically. The substrate is a phosphate ester, not a DNA
  • Any exposed phosphate: 5’ ends of RNA or DNA, dNTPs and NTPs, and small molecules like p-nitrophenyl phosphate — which is why the same enzyme doubles as a colorimetric reporter
  • Take a 5’ phosphate off and you revoke two licences: the end can no longer be ligated, and λ exonuclease will no longer start on it
  • And they cannot tell your vector from your insert — which matters later

Why you phosphatase a vector

Three phosphatases, one difference that matters

  • The difference is not activity. It is whether you can get the enzyme out before the ligation — because a phosphatase that survives strips the phosphates you just paid for

One that heat will not kill

  • CIP — calf intestine alkaline phosphatase. Oldest, cheapest, most robust; the one every protocol names
  • Notoriously not inactivated by heat — you must column-purify or extract it away before you ligate

Two that it does

  • rSAP (recombinant shrimp) and Antarctic Phosphatase — a short heat step inactivates them cleanly, so you can ligate in the same tube
  • rSAP is also half of exo-SAP: SAP destroys the leftover dNTPs, Exonuclease I the leftover primers, before cycle sequencing
