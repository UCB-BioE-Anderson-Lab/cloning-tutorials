THE DETINT, AND THE SHAPE IT MADE DISAPPEAR
-------------------------------------------
recolour_pptx.py maps a theme slot to a palette colour, but a shape that
reaches its colour through a lumMod/lumOff TINT comes out as a pale wash
of it -- source 13's Klenow strands, source 16's NNK labels and the whole
of source 17 were a 40% vermillion that reads as pink on white.  So the
white-ground tree also strips those tints from the slides.

The first pass stripped them from EVERY colour and broke source 37: its
promoter arrow and bent arrow are a TINT OF bg1, which is how this deck
draws grey furniture.  Undo the tint and they become pure white, and the
figure simply loses two shapes -- no error, no warning, and the slide
still looks like a slide.  JCA would have been shown a promoter driving
nothing.

So the rule is: strip a tint off a TEXT or ACCENT slot, keep it on a
BACKGROUND slot (bg1, bg2, lt1, lt2, dk1, tx1).  build_white.py holds it.
Worth re-reading AUTHORING.txt's "art drawn for a grey ground vanishes on
white" next to this: both failures are a shape that is still there, still
the right size, and no longer visible.

THE SPLOTCHES: -opaque IGNORES ALPHA
-------------------------------------
JCA, 4 Nov 2026, walking the deck on localhost, on source 18 and then on
21, 27, 28 and 29: "redownload or redraw that image.  It's splotchy."

He was looking at a defect the inkify pass had introduced, and it is the
kind that only shows at slide size.  `-opaque white` compares RGB and
IGNORES the alpha channel.  A pixel that is white at alpha 0.08 becomes
INK at alpha 0.08, which flattens to a pale grey smudge.  And these
rasters are almost entirely that: measured over the fifteen inked
figures, 78 to 96 PERCENT of every one of them is halo under alpha 0.6.
They are JPEGs wearing a PNG, and the compression ring around every mark
got painted.  Blown up three times from a 500 px scan, the rings read as
dirt.

The fix is one step and it goes FIRST: threshold the alpha channel at
60%, so the halo is gone before anything is painted.

  -channel A -threshold 60% +channel        <- this, then
  -channel RGB -fuzz N% -fill #111 -opaque white +channel
  -background white -flatten

Two things the same pass had got wrong, both found by looking rather than
by any check:

  source 27, the M13 particle.  Its labels were white and wanted inking,
  but a 35% fuzz also paints the white highlight on every pVIII subunit
  and the whole phage goes to a dark blob.  It is inked at 12%, which
  takes the labels and leader lines and leaves the shading.

  source 21, the invasin recovery chart.  It is NOT inked and must not
  be.  Its three series are black bars, grey bars and WHITE bars, and
  its axis labels are white letters with a thin dark outline -- white is
  DATA here, so painting it would both fill the white bars and destroy
  the distinction between the series.  AUTHORING.txt's answer for "white
  marks that ARE data next to black data" is a grey panel, so that
  figure keeps its alpha, is NOT cropped off the render at all, and sits
  on --muted in the section file.  Its legend, which is native shapes
  rather than part of the raster, is set as HTML beside the plot where
  the source puts it.

THE SWEEP THAT FOLLOWED, AND WHAT IT DID NOT FIND
--------------------------------------------------
JCA, 4 Nov 2026: "look for more such issues".  Two sweeps, because the
ink pass can fail in two opposite directions and neither one errors.

TOO LITTLE INK, or smudge left behind.  Every figure was flattened onto
white and measured for the fraction of pixels in the mid-grey band that
clean line art should hardly have, then all 45 were looked at as four
sheets, which is what AUTHORING.txt asks for.  Nothing else was splotchy.

TOO MUCH INK, or a shape that vanished.  A CONTROL tree was rendered --
white ground only, no detint, no inkify -- and diffed against the build
slide by slide: ink present in the control and absent from the build is
a shape the pipeline ate.  Every slide came back clean.  Where the build
has LESS ink than the control (18, 19, 28, 29, 30, at most 2%) it is the
halo that went, and an A/B of those five confirms the build is strictly
more legible.  Re-run that diff after any change to build_white.py; it
is the only check that would have caught the source 37 arrow.

THE RENDER TREE IS A SCRIPT, NOT A SEQUENCE OF COMMANDS
-------------------------------------------------------
build_white.py (beside this file) rebuilds wh/ from rc/unpacked in one go:
white <p:bg> into every slide, the selective detint, the shape drops, and
the per-figure inkify.  It had to be run SEVEN times while this deck was
converted -- every time a figure turned out to be white line art, once
when the detint went too far, once for a label that sat inside a figure's
box, and once for the alpha bug above -- and each run has to produce the
SAME tree, or the crop boxes in crops.txt stop meaning anything.  Then:

  cd wh && zip -qr ../white.pptx . -x '.*'
  soffice --headless --convert-to pdf white.pptx --outdir ..
  pdftoppm -png -r 200 white.pdf wpages/w

and crops.txt is replayed against wpages/.  (zip run with cwd inside the
tree resolves a RELATIVE archive path against that cwd, which is how the
Whole-Cell deck once re-rendered the old bytes; pass an absolute path.)

A NOTE ON REPLAYING crops.txt.  Four divider photographs and the source
21 chart are NOT in that list -- they are copied straight out of the
pptx because they need their alpha.  An earlier replay overwrote all
four with white-ground crops and put a white card back on the amber
dividers.  crops.txt says so at the bottom; read it before replaying.

THE ONE WORD THAT WAS CHANGED, AND WHY IT IS NOT A CONVERSION EDIT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Source 6's second bullet read "how many different permutations of these
sequences are there?"  A permutation OF a sequence is a rearrangement of
it, so read literally the bullet asks for the 720 shuffles of Seq1.  What
the slide computes is 20^6: ordered, WITH repetition, which is why Seq2
and Seq3 reuse black.  The bullet now reads

    how many different sequences are possible?

JCA made the call on 2026-11-10.  The change was made in the SOURCE .pptx
first (ppt/slides/slide6.xml, the one visible run) and then in the HTML, so
the two do not drift.  The SPOKEN note is untouched: it still says
"permutations", and so does the next build's note ("there are 20^6
permutations"), which is the ordinary loose usage and contradicts nothing.
verify_notes.py therefore still passes unchanged -- the notes channel is
verbatim as always, and only the visible bullet moved.

1. SOURCE 6'S BULLET.  "how many different permutations of these
   sequences are there?" -> "how many different sequences are
   possible?"  A permutation OF a sequence is a rearrangement of it,
   so read literally it asked for the 720 shuffles of Seq1.  Changed
   in the .pptx first (ppt/slides/slide6.xml) and then here.

2. THE LOTTERY REFRAME, sources 5, 6 and 8.  The gumball machine is
   now a lottery draw.  Same arithmetic, a more familiar picture.
   Source 6 is animated: seq/06-lottery.js runs a globe of churning
   balls and six roll out of the chute on a click.
   THE TRAP, and the reason the bullet says what it says: a real
   lottery draws WITHOUT replacement, which gives P(20,6) = 27,907,200
   and not 20^6.  So the globe holds MANY balls of each of twenty
   numbers and every ball is replaced -- the gumball machine's premise
   kept exactly -- and the slide says so in words.  Seq2 and Seq3
   repeat numbers on purpose.
   The spoken notes follow the artwork, through note-corrections.txt,
   in a block clearly marked as not-typos.

2b. SOURCE 8 IS A LOTTERY TICKET, not a hexamer.  One draw of six
   numbers is one ticket, and the question is "how many random tickets
   do I have to buy to have a 99% chance that I hold the winning one?"
   THIS IS ALSO THE MORE ACCURATE QUESTION.  P = 1 - e^(-p/n) = 0.99
   gives p = 4.6n for ONE named member -- the winning ticket.  The
   source's second bullet asked for "at least one of each theoretical
   member", which is the coupon-collector problem and needs n.ln(n),
   about 18n here, not 4.6n.  The slide's own lede always said "a
   single member", so the formula and the lede agreed and only the
   bullet overstated it.  The reframe fixes that by accident and the
   slide is now self-consistent.
   The ticket is drawn in seq/balls.js (data-ticket), and the slide
   gained one muted line putting a number on it: n = 6.4e7 possible
   tickets, so p = 2.9e8 tickets.  That line is new to the deck and
   carries data-note-add rather than a verbatim note.

3. SOURCE 7.  Five bullets became three, tightened, and the slide
   gained the calculation drawn out: a 1 L flask, five ten-fold
   dilutions, 100 uL plated from the last three, and plates of 160, 15
   and 2 colonies.  Only the 160 plate is in the countable range, and
   the arithmetic rests on it alone: 160 / 0.1 mL x 10^3 x 1000 mL =
   1.6e9 transformants.  seq/07-titer.js, which carries the numbers
   and the reasoning.
