WHY NO WHITE-GROUND RENDER WAS NEEDED
--------------------------------------
Unlike lectures 10 and 11, this source deck already puts its content on
WHITE.  Only the title and the section dividers use the blue master.  So
the figures are cropped straight from a render of the untouched pptx --
no recolour_pptx.py, no build_white.py, no inking pass, and therefore
none of the halo and speckle problems those passes caused in lecture 11.

The crops are the union of every picture's bounding box on a slide, read
from each p:pic's a:xfrm and converted at 200 dpi (EMU / 4572 on this
9144000 x 6858000 page).  Anchor on a:xfrm, never on .//a:ext -- a:ext
is also the extension-list element and matches first.

DEEP MUTATIONAL SCANNING WAS ALREADY HERE, UNNAMED
---------------------------------------------------
2026-11-16.  I told JCA that deep mutational scanning was missing from
Protein Function and should be added.  That was wrong, and the way it
was wrong decided the design of the fix.  SOURCE 31 IS PMID 20711194 --
Fowler et al., Nat Methods 2010 -- which is the paper that FOUNDED deep
mutational scanning and named it.  It was already in the deck, framed
as a thing you can do to a phage display experiment rather than as the
start of a method.

So the two new slides go immediately AFTER 31 and give that experiment
its name, rather than introducing the method cold somewhere else:

  seq/31-dms.js     "Deep mutational scanning".  The five-step pipeline
                    down the left, and the position-by-amino-acid map
                    it produces on the right, with four columns picked
                    out to teach you how such a map reads.  4 steps.
  seq/31b-brca1.js  "Variants of uncertain significance".  Findlay
                    et al., Nature 2018, PMID 30209399: the VUS problem,
                    saturation genome editing of BRCA1, and the bimodal
                    distribution that makes the variants classifiable.
                    4 steps.

TWO NUMBERS THAT MUST NOT BE MERGED.  Fowler tracked ">600,000
variants" of a WW domain, but a WW domain is about 35 residues, so its
single substitutions number in the hundreds -- his library carried
multiples as well.  The slide says "every substitution at every
position" for the MODERN form of the method and gives 600,000
separately as Fowler's own figure.

THE HEATMAP AND THE HISTOGRAM ARE BOTH SCHEMATIC and both say so.  What
is faithful in the heatmap is how it READS; the two conserved
tryptophans are real, because a WW domain is named for them.  What is
faithful in the histogram is its SHAPE -- bimodal with a sparse valley
-- and the counts printed beside it are the paper's own.

THE CLOSER ON THE BRCA1 SLIDE IS DELIBERATELY HEDGED.  The paper says
"We predict that these results will be immediately useful for the
clinical interpretation of BRCA1 variants".  That is a prediction about
utility, not a report that any clinic adopted the data, and the note
says so in those terms.  JCA pulled me up earlier in this deck for
claiming variants "actually go into patient reports", which I could not
support; do not let that claim back in.

CHECKING THE NOTES
------------------
  python3 ../../tools/verify_notes.py <unpacked-pptx> --keep-urls \
      --corrections note-corrections.txt *.html

  <unpacked-pptx> is a directory the pptx has been unzipped into:
      mkdir -p /tmp/mx && cd /tmp/mx && unzip -o "<the pptx>"

READ THE LINE ABOVE BEFORE RUNNING ANYTHING.  On 2026-11-17 this was
ignored and the check was run as

      python3 ../../tools/verify_notes.py .            # WRONG

for a whole working session, by one session and four agents.  That form
EXITS 0 AND PRINTS NOTHING.  argv[1] is the unpacked pptx, not the deck,
and the html files are the arguments after it -- so passing "." gives it
a pptx root that contains no slides and a list of zero files to check.
It does not error.  It reports success at checking nothing.

Every "notes verify" claimed that day was worthless.  The deck did turn
out to be clean when the check was finally run properly, but that was
luck, not evidence.

HOW TO KNOW THE CHECK IS REALLY RUNNING: it prints one "source N: ok"
line per slide, 42 of them for this deck.  Silence means it checked
NOTHING.  If you want to be sure, corrupt a note on purpose, confirm it
reports MISMATCH, and put it back.

the deck's credit slot:

  TRMR is a method developed by Ryan Gill that involves the generation of genomic mutations from oligonucleotides.
  These oligos are generated from chips and designed to target specific sites in specific genes with mutations and a barcode.
  The procedure to introduce these mutations involve MAGE-like reactions involving homologous recombination enhanced by lambda red.
  The result is a library of strains in which up and down mutations to the genes are tagged with barcodes.
  Typically there is one such modification to each genome.
  This population of bacteria is subjected to selections such as a challenge with a toxic metabolite.
  Those mutations that enhance performance under these conditions become enriched while those that reduce performance are diminished.
  After selection, the barcodes are PCR’d out of the sample and subjected to deep sequencing.
  Based on the observed frequency of each barcode, the relevance of each mutation to a growth challenge can be inferred.

If TRMR ever comes back, this is its note.
