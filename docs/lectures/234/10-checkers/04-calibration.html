Every gene here already works

4,310 coding sequences from E. coli K-12 MG1655. All of them are transcribed, translated and load-bearing.

So every gene your checker flags is, by construction, a false positive.

That is what a calibration set is: not sequences you hope are right, sequences whose answer you already know by a route the checker had no part in.

How many real genes does that cutoff reject?

What the curve is telling you

  • The intuitive answer was wrong by a factor of twenty-five
  • And it was wrong silently — the code runs, the tests pass
  • The cliff between 8 and 12 means feel is not precise enough to pick with
  • You cannot defend a threshold whose consequences you have not looked at

And the other half of the problem

A cutoff of 16 flags nothing. It also catches nothing.

The false-positive rate is only one side. A checker that never fires is perfectly calibrated and perfectly useless, and this genome cannot tell you that — it has no true positives in it to miss.

For that you need sequences that should fail: known internal promoters, or a promoter family whose strengths were measured. That is the J23100 set, and it is where L12 picks this up.
