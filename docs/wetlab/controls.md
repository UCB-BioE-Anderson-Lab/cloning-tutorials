# Control Strains and Plasmids

A plate with no colonies on it can mean four different things: the DNA was bad, the cells were
not competent, the plates do not select, or something else went wrong. Without controls there is
no way to tell which. The **control stocks box** in the **−80 freezer** holds what you need to
find out.

## When you need them

The carbenicillin plates stocked in the fridge are already proven. When you select with
**anything other than carbenicillin**, the plates were poured for your experiment, so they are as
unproven as your transformation. Set up all three controls beside your real plates.

## The three controls

Each control answers a different question, so none of them can stand in for another.

| Control | What you do | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Restreak** | Streak the **control strain** onto one of your plates | The plates select at all. A batch poured with dead antibiotic looks exactly like a failed transformation. |
| **Positive** | Transform the **control plasmid** into the **same competent cells** | The cells are competent and took up DNA. |
| **Negative** | The **same competent cells**, with **no DNA** added | The plate is not just growing untransformed cells. |

The restreak is not the positive control. The positive tests the cells; the restreak tests the
plates.

## Getting them out of the freezer

The box is on the **top shelf of the −80**, a green box labelled `control_stocks`. Leave it in the
freezer until you are ready, and don't hold the freezer door open while you look.

- Take out the **plasmid** tubes you need for your transformation.
- Take out a **cell stock** only when you are about to streak it, and put it straight back. It must
  not thaw.

## Doing the restreak

1. Touch a pipette tip to the frozen surface of the cell stock. You only need a little, but you
   should be able to see cells on the tip.
2. Spread it across the plate.
3. Put the stock back in the box, and the box back in the −80.

The negative control plate gets **competent cells with no DNA**. Follow your lab sheet for which
cells to use. In the video the negative plate is left empty; that is a mistake, corrected on
screen.

## What is in the box

Samples are named by the antibiotic they select on: **A** for carbenicillin/ampicillin, **K** for
kanamycin, **S** for spectinomycin, **E** for erythromycin. The tube label is what is written on
the tube itself.

| Sample | Plasmid | Tube label | Selects on |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A1` | pJ01 | `pJ01-C 2°` | carbenicillin / ampicillin |
| `A2` | pJ12 | `pJ12-A 2°` | carbenicillin / ampicillin |
| `A3` | pJ19 | `pJ19-A 2°` | carbenicillin / ampicillin |
| `K1` | p20N31 | `p20N31 2°` | kanamycin |
| `K2` | pKQ-Upp | `pKQ-Upp` | kanamycin |
| `S1` | pTargetF | `pTargetF` | spectinomycin |
| `E1` | pTRKH3-slpGFP | `pTRKH3-slpGFP` | erythromycin |

pJ01, pJ12 and pJ19 express GFP from promoters of different strengths: pJ12 is barely green,
pJ01 medium, pJ19 bright.

## Reading the plates

| Restreak | Positive | Negative | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| grows | grows | no growth | Controls worked. Your own plates can be trusted. |
| no growth | — | — | Nothing could have grown on this batch of plates, so a blank plate from it tells you nothing. |
| grows | no growth | no growth | The cells are not competent. |
| grows | grows | grows | The plates are not killing untransformed cells. |

Two more things to check:

- **A lawn instead of colonies.** If a transformation plate is covered edge to edge, nothing was
  selected: the cells were already resistant to that antibiotic. Mach1 is already resistant to
  erythromycin, so erythromycin experiments need a different strain. Use the one your lab sheet
  names.
- **Colour under blue light.** Colonies should look the way their plasmid predicts. The `K1`
  control carries GFP and glows; `S1` (pTargetF) has none and stays white. A plate of white
  colonies where you expected green ones means something else grew.
