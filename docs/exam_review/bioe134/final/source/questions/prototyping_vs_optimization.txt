difficulty: easy
topic: Distinguishing prototyping from optimization
question: |
  What is the key practical difference between a prototyping experiment and an optimization experiment?
choices:
  A: Prototyping tunes expression levels, while optimization avoids any genetic changes.
  B: Prototyping analyzes LCMS files, while optimization performs only DNA sequencing.
  C: Prototyping starts from existing DNA, while optimization ignores prior constructs.
  D: Prototyping builds a new design, while optimization edits an existing prototype.
answer: D
explanation: |
  Prototyping is used when there is no existing DNA for the design, so the construct is built from scratch. Optimization starts from a prototype or CurrBest and makes edits to improve it. C reverses the relationship, B confuses build and analysis tasks, and A is too narrow and partly wrong.

slug: hard_wrong_optimization_plan
difficulty: hard
topic: Diagnosing a flawed optimization plan
question: |
  A team has a working three-gene prototype that produces a low but measurable amount of product. They propose this next step:

  "Discard the prototype, synthesize a completely new three-gene construct from scratch with different promoters and orthologs for all genes, and compare it only to the old LCMS file."

  What is the main problem with calling this an optimization experiment?
choices:
  A: It changes too many factors at once and loses the incremental comparison to CurrBest.
  B: It uses LCMS, which the lecture defines as appropriate only during initial prototyping.
  C: It includes promoter edits, which cannot be applied after a prototype is confirmed.
  D: It compares constructs across cycles, which is not valid once any product is detected.
answer: A
explanation: |
  Optimization is most useful when it makes interpretable incremental edits relative to CurrBest. Changing promoters and orthologs for all genes at once makes it hard to attribute any change in performance and gives up the efficiency of editing the existing prototype. B is wrong because LCMS is central to testing. C is false because promoter changes are common optimization edits. D is wrong because comparison is the point of optimization.

slug: plateau_and_theoretical_limits
difficulty: medium
topic: When optimization becomes less useful
question: |
  The slides describe optimization as "money in the bank until theoretical limits reached/plateau." Which interpretation is most accurate?
choices:
  A: Optimization always gives the same improvement after every edit.
  B: Optimization is unnecessary once any detectable product is observed.
  C: Optimization can improve performance, but gains eventually diminish.
  D: Optimization guarantees that theoretical maximum yield is reached.
answer: C
explanation: |
  Early optimization often has many possible improvements, such as relieving bottlenecks or balancing expression. As the design improves, remaining gains may become smaller or limited by biology. A and D overstate predictability, and B stops too early because detectable product is often only a starting point.
