  In a graph representation of a molecule, what do the nodes and edges usually represent?
choices:
  A: Nodes are bonds, and edges are atoms.

slug: smiles_vs_inchi_readability
difficulty: easy
topic: SMILES and InChI serializations
question: |
  Why might a person prefer SMILES over InChI when writing a molecule by hand?
choices:
  A: SMILES directly encodes reaction rates for enzymes.
  B: SMILES is more human-readable and writable.
  C: SMILES guarantees one unique string per molecule.
  D: SMILES removes the need for a molecular graph.
answer: B
explanation: |
  SMILES is designed to be relatively human-readable and writable. InChI is useful because it is deterministic and behaves more like a structural identifier, but it is not usually convenient to write manually. Neither format directly encodes reaction rates, and both can be interpreted through molecular graphs.

  A: They represent only one exact molecule with fixed stereochemistry.
  B: They replace the need to conserve atoms in reactions.
  C: They describe families of related molecules using variable groups.

topic: Standardizing chemically equivalent representations
question: |
  A synthesis algorithm stores one molecule in its enol form and another database stores the equivalent keto form. What problem can this create if structures are not standardized?
choices:
  A: The algorithm may fail to recognize equivalent chemicals as matching.
  B: The algorithm must convert every reaction into a PCR protocol.
  C: The molecule will lose all stereochemical information permanently.
  D: The molecule cannot be represented by any graph or string.
answer: A
explanation: |
  Tautomers can be different representations of related or equivalent chemical forms. If a synthesis algorithm does not standardize these representations, it may treat the same chemical entity as different entries and miss matches between reactions, metabolites, or products. The other options introduce unrelated or overly absolute claims.
