difficulty: easy
topic: Stochastic simulations use molecule counts
question: |
  What distinguishes a stochastic reaction simulation from a deterministic mass action model?
choices:
  A: It tracks discrete reaction events and molecule counts.
  B: It stores only the final reachable product list.
  C: It replaces reactions with ConstructionFile steps.
  D: It ignores rate constants and reactant abundance.
answer: A
explanation: |
  Stochastic simulations model individual reaction events and discrete molecule counts. Deterministic models usually track continuous concentrations. Stochastic simulations still use rate constants and reactant abundance; they do not replace reactions with ConstructionFile steps or reachable lists.

slug: propensity_basic_meaning
difficulty: easy
topic: Reaction propensity
question: |
  In a stochastic simulation, what does a reaction propensity represent?
choices:
  A: The chromatographic retention time of a product.
  B: The likelihood that a reaction fires next.
  C: The freezer location of a reaction mixture.
  D: The DNA sequence of the encoded enzyme.
answer: B
explanation: |
  Propensity represents the instantaneous tendency of a reaction to occur, usually depending on the rate constant and the number or concentration of required reactants. It is a kinetic simulation quantity, not an LCMS, inventory, or sequence field.

question: |
  In a stochastic model, reaction 1 consumes molecule A. If the count of A increases while the rate constant stays the same, what usually happens to the reaction propensity?
choices:
  A: It increases because more reactant molecules are available.
  B: It decreases because products become more concentrated.
  C: It becomes zero because stochastic models avoid rates.
  D: It becomes unrelated to the reaction rate constant.
answer: A
explanation: |
  For mass-action-style stochastic propensities, more available reactant molecules generally increase the likelihood that the reaction occurs. The propensity still depends on the rate constant. Product concentration is not the reason a forward consuming reaction becomes more likely.

  What does the update matrix specify in a stochastic biochemical reaction simulation?
choices:
  A: The LCMS peak areas for all pathway metabolites.
  B: The net molecule-count changes caused by each reaction.
  C: The primer pairs used to construct each pathway enzyme.
  D: The order in which samples are loaded onto a robot deck.

slug: update_vector_application
difficulty: medium
topic: Applying an update vector
question: |
  A reaction is:

  ```
  A + B -> C
  ```

  If this reaction fires once in a stochastic simulation, which molecule-count update is correct?
choices:
  A: A increases by 1, B increases by 1, C decreases by 1.
  B: A decreases by 1, B increases by 1, C decreases by 1.
  C: A decreases by 1, B decreases by 1, C increases by 1.
  D: A stays constant, B decreases by 1, C decreases by 1.
answer: C
explanation: |
  One firing of A + B -> C consumes one A and one B and produces one C. The update vector is therefore -1 for A, -1 for B, and +1 for C.

slug: propensity_vs_update_matrix
difficulty: hard
topic: Propensities versus update matrix
question: |
  A model has two reactions:

  ```
  R1: A -> B
  R2: A -> C
  ```

  R1 and R2 have the same update magnitude for A because both consume one A. However, R1 has a much larger propensity than R2. What does that mean?
choices:
  A: R1 consumes more A per firing because its propensity is larger.
  B: R2 cannot fire because its update vector matches R1 for A.
  C: R1 is more likely to fire next, but both reactions consume one A if they fire.
  D: The update matrix must be ignored when propensities differ.
answer: C
explanation: |
  Propensity determines how likely a reaction is to occur next. The update vector determines what happens if that reaction fires. A larger propensity for R1 means it is more likely to be selected, not that it consumes more A per firing.

slug: stochastic_toggle_noise
difficulty: hard
topic: Why stochasticity matters in genetic circuits
question: |
  Why might a stochastic model be useful for a genetic circuit with low molecule counts?
choices:
  A: Low molecule counts make every reaction deterministic and smooth.
  B: Stochastic models remove the need to specify reaction networks.
  C: Genetic circuits cannot be represented with species and reactions.
  D: Random reaction timing can affect circuit state when counts are small.
answer: D
explanation: |
  In low-copy systems, individual reaction events can matter, and random timing can change the behavior of the circuit. Stochastic models are useful for capturing this discreteness. They still require species and reactions, and they do not make dynamics automatically smooth.
