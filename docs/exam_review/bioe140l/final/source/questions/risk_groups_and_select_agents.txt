slug: what_risk_groups_describe
difficulty: easy
topic: Understanding what a risk group classification means
question: |
  What does an organism's risk group describe?
choices:
  A: The hazard it presents to the individuals working with it
  B: The probability that it will escape the laboratory entirely
  C: The chemical hazard of the compounds it is engineered to make
  D: The monetary value of the research it is being used for
  E: The level of government approval its use requires in writing
answer: A
explanation: |
  The risk groups rank organisms by the hazard they pose in a laboratory exposure, from agents unlikely to cause disease in healthy adults up through those causing severe disease with no available treatment. Containment requirements follow from the classification rather than defining it, and the chemistry of what the organism produces is assessed separately.

slug: risk_group_of_a_construct
difficulty: easy
topic: Classifying an engineered strain
question: |
  How is the risk group of an engineered strain determined?
choices:
  A: By the host strain alone, since the inserts are not organisms
  B: By the worst of the host and whatever has been put into it
  C: By averaging the risk groups of the host and the inserts
  D: By the containment level of the laboratory it is used in
  E: By the risk group of the organism the insert came from
answer: B
explanation: |
  Taking a harmless host and giving it a virulence determinant produces something more hazardous than the host was, so the classification has to account for both the chassis and its cargo and settles on the more serious of them. Neither the host alone nor the donor alone decides it, and the laboratory's containment is what the classification obliges rather than what sets it.

slug: chemical_hazard_is_not_biological
difficulty: medium
topic: Separating chemical from biological hazard
question: |
  A non-pathogenic E. coli is engineered to make a flammable hydrocarbon using genes from soil bacteria. What is its risk group?
choices:
  A: RG2, because the product presents a hazard to workers
  B: A select agent, because fuels have potential for misuse
  C: RG1, because no biological hazard has been added
  D: RG3, because flammable products require stronger containment
  E: It cannot be classified until the product is characterized
answer: C
explanation: |
  The host is harmless and the donors are non-pathogenic, so nothing has been added that makes the organism more dangerous to the people handling it. The flammability of the product is a real hazard and is managed as a chemical one, with the appropriate storage and fire precautions, but it does not move the strain's biological classification.

slug: adding_a_virulence_factor
difficulty: medium
topic: Reasoning about a virulence determinant in a safe host
question: |
  A routine cloning strain is engineered to display a functional adhesin that lets it attach to and invade human epithelial cells. How should it be classified?
choices:
  A: RG1, since the parent cloning strain is itself harmless
  B: RG3, since the donor organism causes serious disease
  C: A select agent, because an invasion gene has been moved
  D: RG2, since the added function raises what an exposure could do
  E: Unclassifiable, since adhesins act only on cultured cell lines
answer: D
explanation: |
  The chassis was innocuous and now carries a function that lets it enter human cells, which changes what an accidental exposure could lead to and so lifts the classification above the host's own. It does not simply inherit the donor's classification, since one determinant is not the whole pathogen, and nothing in the description raises the deliberate-misuse concern that defines a select agent.

slug: what_makes_a_select_agent
difficulty: medium
topic: Understanding the select agent concept
question: |
  What distinguishes a select agent from an organism that is merely high risk group?
choices:
  A: Its requirement for a biosafety cabinet during handling
  B: Its resistance to the antibiotics used in the laboratory
  C: Its potential for deliberate misuse against people or crops
  D: Its ability to survive outside the laboratory environment
  E: Its classification by the institution rather than by statute
answer: C
explanation: |
  Risk group speaks to accidental harm to a worker, while the select agent lists exist because an agent could be used deliberately to cause mass harm, which brings registration, inventory control and personnel vetting with it. Containment equipment, drug resistance and environmental persistence may accompany such agents without being what puts them on the list.

slug: misuse_potential_classification
difficulty: hard
topic: Classifying an agent by its misuse potential
question: |
  A plant pathogen capable of destroying a staple cereal crop is on a federal list requiring registration, inventory control and personnel vetting. What does that status reflect?
choices:
  A: That it is classified in the highest risk group available
  B: That no treatment exists for the disease it causes
  C: That it is assessed as a target for deliberate misuse
  D: That it requires the strictest containment to handle safely
  E: That it cannot be transferred between institutions at all
answer: C
explanation: |
  Registration, inventory control and vetting of the people with access are the controls attached to agents judged capable of being used deliberately to cause mass harm, to people, livestock or crops. That is a different question from how dangerous the organism is to the person handling it, which is what risk group and containment level describe, and an agent can be hazardous in a laboratory without appearing on such a list.

slug: risk_group_versus_containment_level
difficulty: hard
topic: Distinguishing risk group from biosafety level
question: |
  How do risk group and biosafety level differ?
choices:
  A: Risk group ranks the agent; biosafety level describes the practices
  B: They are the same scale under two different names
  C: Risk group applies to bacteria and biosafety level to viruses
  D: Risk group is assigned locally and biosafety level nationally
  E: Risk group covers accidents and biosafety level covers misuse
answer: A
explanation: |
  One classifies the agent and the other prescribes how work with it is conducted: the facility, the equipment and the procedures. They usually correspond, but not always, because the containment required also depends on what is being done, so a procedure generating aerosols with a lower risk group agent may be carried out at a higher containment level.

slug: host_attenuation_and_classification
difficulty: hard
topic: Reasoning about a deliberately weakened host
question: |
  Why are laboratory E. coli strains such as the common cloning hosts treated as low risk?
choices:
  A: Because they cannot take up foreign DNA from the environment
  B: Because they carry mutations leaving them poorly able to colonize
  C: Because they are killed by the antibiotics used in the medium
  D: Because they grow only at temperatures below body temperature
  E: Because they lack the ability to replicate plasmids efficiently
answer: B
explanation: |
  Decades of laboratory passage and deliberate modification have left these strains dependent on rich medium and poor at colonizing a human gut, which is a large part of why routine cloning is low risk work. They take up DNA readily, which is the point of them, replicate plasmids well, and grow perfectly happily at body temperature.

slug: classifying_before_the_work_begins
difficulty: hard
topic: Knowing when classification must happen
question: |
  At what point should an engineered strain's risk classification be settled?
choices:
  A: After the first results indicate whether it is hazardous
  B: Before the work begins, as part of the approval for it
  C: At publication, when the construct is described in full
  D: Whenever an exposure incident occurs in the laboratory
  E: Only if the strain is to be transferred to another laboratory
answer: B
explanation: |
  The classification determines where the work may be done, what equipment is required and what training the people need, all of which have to be in place before anyone handles the organism. Deciding afterward means the work has already been done at whatever containment happened to be convenient, and an incident is the worst possible moment to discover the answer.

slug: reclassifying_after_a_modification
difficulty: hard
topic: Recognizing when a modification changes the classification
question: |
  Which modification to an RG1 host would most clearly require reassessment of its classification?
choices:
  A: Codon optimizing an existing gene for better expression
  B: Replacing the plasmid origin with a higher copy number one
  C: Adding a gene conferring resistance to a clinical antibiotic
  D: Adding a fluorescent protein for tracking the strain
  E: Adding a terminator downstream of the reporter gene
answer: C
explanation: |
  Resistance to a drug used to treat human infection changes what an exposure would mean and how an infection could be managed, which is a change in hazard rather than in performance. Codon changes, copy number, reporters and terminators all alter how the construct behaves without altering what harm the organism could do to a person.
