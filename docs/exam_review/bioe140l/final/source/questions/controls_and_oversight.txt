slug: classifying_a_control_measure
difficulty: easy
topic: Classifying control measures by type
question: |
  A rule requires two people to sign the inventory before a regulated strain leaves the freezer. What kind of control is that?
choices:
  A: An engineering control, since the freezer restricts access
  B: Personal protective equipment for the people handling it
  C: An administrative control, being a procedure governing the work
  D: A biological containment measure applied to the strain
  E: A procedural control carried out during the experiment
answer: C
explanation: |
  Administrative controls are the policies that govern who may do what and under what conditions, and a required countersignature is exactly that: it constrains the work rather than containing the hazard. A lock on the freezer would be an engineering control addressing the same concern, and the distinction matters because the two fail in different ways.

question: |
  A synthesis company checks incoming orders against sequences of concern. What kind of control is that?
choices:
  A: An engineering control, since it is performed by software
  B: An administrative control, being a policy applied to the work
  C: Personal protective equipment for the people synthesizing
  D: A procedural control applied after the synthesis is complete
  E: A biological containment measure built into the product
answer: B
explanation: |
  Screening is a policy that governs what work is accepted, applied before anything is made, which puts it with training and standard procedures among the administrative controls. That it is implemented in software does not make it an engineering control, since engineering controls are physical features of the workplace such as cabinets and ventilation.

slug: engineering_control_examples
difficulty: medium
topic: Identifying engineering controls
question: |
  Which pair are both engineering controls?
choices:
  A: A biosafety cabinet and a kill switch in the organism
  B: A respirator and a standard operating procedure
  C: Required training and an autoclave cycle for waste
  D: A laboratory coat and a signed use authorization
  E: Gloves and a sequence screening policy for orders
answer: A
explanation: |
  Engineering controls are features built into the equipment or the organism so that safety does not rely on someone remembering: a cabinet that contains aerosols, interlocks, directional airflow, and biological containment such as a kill switch or an auxotrophy. Respirators, coats and gloves protect the individual wearing them, and training, procedures and screening are administrative.

slug: hierarchy_of_controls
difficulty: medium
topic: Reasoning about why control types are ranked
question: |
  Why is an engineering control generally preferred to personal protective equipment for the same hazard?
choices:
  A: It costs less to install than the equipment costs to supply
  B: It protects without depending on correct use each time
  C: It is required by regulation where equipment is optional
  D: It removes the need for any administrative controls
  E: It protects the individual rather than the whole laboratory
answer: B
explanation: |
  Protective equipment works only when it is worn, worn properly and changed when it should be, which puts a human decision between the hazard and the person every single time. A cabinet or an interlock contains the hazard whether or not anyone is paying attention, which is why the hierarchy puts it higher, and it protects everyone in the room rather than one wearer.

  What is a biological use authorization?

slug: procedural_versus_administrative
difficulty: hard
topic: Distinguishing a procedural step from a policy
question: |
  Autoclaving waste is often called a procedural control rather than an administrative one. What is the distinction?
choices:
  A: It is performed by equipment rather than by a person
  B: It applies to waste rather than to the live organism
  C: It is a step within the work, not a policy governing it
  D: It is optional, whereas administrative controls are required
  E: It is verified by indicators, whereas policies are not
answer: C
explanation: |
  Administrative controls decide what work may be done, by whom, and under what conditions, while a procedural control is an operation inside that work, carried out at the bench each time. The requirement that waste be autoclaved is itself administrative; the autoclaving is the procedure that satisfies it, which is why the two are easy to run together.

slug: controls_for_an_aerosol_risk
difficulty: hard
topic: Choosing controls for a specific hazard
question: |
  A procedure on an RG2 organism generates aerosols. Which combination addresses that?
choices:
  A: Gloves and a laboratory coat, changed between procedures
  B: Training in the procedure, plus autoclaving of the waste
  C: A cabinet containing the aerosol, with procedure and training
  D: A kill switch engineered into the organism being handled
  E: A signed use authorization naming the procedure performed
answer: C
explanation: |
  The hazard is airborne, so the first line is a physical barrier that captures the aerosol at source, supported by a written procedure for working in it and training in that procedure. Gloves and a coat do not protect against inhalation, waste handling addresses a different route, a kill switch addresses release rather than exposure, and an authorization permits the work without containing anything.

slug: control_that_fails_safe
difficulty: hard
topic: Reasoning about dependence on human compliance
question: |
  Which control continues to protect if the person performing the work forgets a step?
choices:
  A: A standard operating procedure describing the correct steps
  B: Required annual training in handling the organism
  C: A record kept of each procedure as it is performed
  D: A policy requiring waste to be autoclaved before disposal
  E: A cabinet whose airflow contains material regardless
answer: E
explanation: |
  This is the reason the hierarchy exists. Procedures, training, policies and records all work through someone doing the right thing, and they fail together the moment attention lapses. Directional airflow in a cabinet keeps containing material whether or not anyone is thinking about it, which is what makes an engineering control the more robust answer to the same hazard.

difficulty: hard
topic: Connecting order screening to misuse concerns
question: |
  Why do synthesis providers screen orders rather than relying on the purchaser's institution?
choices:
  A: Because institutions lack the sequence databases required
  B: Because screening is cheaper to perform at the provider
  C: Because institutional committees review only published work
  D: Because synthesis is the point where a sequence becomes material
  E: Because purchasers are not obliged to disclose their intent
answer: D
explanation: |
  A hazardous sequence is only information until someone makes DNA out of it, so the synthesis step is the natural chokepoint at which a control can act, and it catches orders that never pass any institutional review. That is a reason of position rather than of capability, and the control works alongside institutional oversight rather than in place of it.
