Picking Colonies

picking

Protocol

  1. Photograph the plates twice — under blue-light transillumination and under ambient light. Save both. Once you have picked, these are the only record of what the colonies looked like.
  2. Tubes or a block. Up to 4 cultures: individual snap-cap culture tubes. More than that: a 24-well block.
  3. Label. Tubes: the clone ID on each tube. Block: the labels go on the airpore sheet, not the block — the wells are identified by position.
  4. Fill each tube or well with 4 mL 2YT + antibiotic — the one on your labsheet. It is not always carb.
  5. Pick 4 colonies from each plate: touch a sterile toothpick to a single colony and drop it in. It stays there.
    • Into a block, pick in labsheet order, left to right. The layout has to match the data-entry layout — if it does not, nothing errors, the results are just silently scrambled. Into labelled tubes it does not matter; the label travels with the culture.
    • Well-isolated colonies only. Never a small one beside a big one; that is a satellite and usually has no plasmid.
    • Do not pick by brightness. Choosing the brightest and then correlating against a prediction is circular.
  6. Cover. Tubes: caps on loosely, so they can breathe. Block: the airpore sheet.
  7. Grow in the shaking incubator, 16–36 h. Into the fridge by 36 h — past that they overgrow.
  8. Parafilm the plates and store them upside-down in the fridge — you may need to go back to them.
  • Note why you picked each one (“very green, slow growing”).
