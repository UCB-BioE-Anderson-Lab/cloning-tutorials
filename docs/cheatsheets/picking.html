Picking Colonies

picking

Protocol

  1. Photograph the plates twice — under blue-light transillumination and under ambient light. Save both. Once you have picked, these are the only record of what the colonies looked like.
  2. Tubes or a block. Up to 4 cultures: snap-cap tubes, each labelled with its clone ID. More than that: a 24-well block, labels on the airpore sheet — the wells are identified by position.
  3. Fill each tube or well with 4 mL 2YT + antibiotic — the one on your labsheet. It is not always carb.
  4. Pick 4 colonies from each plate: touch a sterile toothpick to a single colony and drop it in. It stays there.
    • Pick the ones that are easy to pick and look healthy — well separated, round and smooth. Big and well isolated is usually best.
    • Avoid contorted or smudgy colonies. That look means water has seeped across the plate, or two colonies have grown into one.
    • Which colonies to choose is set by the experiment — brightest, smallest, largest, red, or deliberately naive. Your labsheet says which.
    • Into a block, follow the grid on your labsheet. Wells are identified by position, so a block filled in a different order is silently scrambled — nothing errors. Labelled tubes do not have this problem.
  5. Cover. Tubes: caps on loosely, so they can breathe. Block: the airpore sheet.
  6. Grow in the shaking incubator, 16–36 h. Into the fridge by 36 h — past that they overgrow.
  7. Parafilm the plates and store them upside-down in the fridge — you may need to go back to them.
